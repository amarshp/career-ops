# Resume Norms: Canada
Last updated: 2026-05-29
Source: IRCC Express Entry guidelines, Canadian tech recruiter practice, Glassdoor CA salary data

## Mandatory inclusions

- **Work authorization / immigration status note**: Unlike the US, Canada does not have EEOC-equivalent restrictions on mentioning immigration status. Include:
  > "Require employer LMIA exemption or Express Entry-aligned work permit sponsorship."
  OR more simply: "Eligible to work in Canada with employer-sponsored work permit."
- **Current location + relocation intent**: "Bengaluru, India — open to relocation to Toronto / Vancouver / Canada."
- **LinkedIn URL**: Critical — Canadian tech recruiters (especially Toronto and Vancouver) use LinkedIn as the primary screening tool.
- **GitHub URL**: Important for AI/ML roles.

## Prohibited / omit

- **Photo**: Never. Canadian hiring norms follow North American practice — no photos.
- **Date of birth / age**: Never.
- **Marital status / religion / ethnicity**: Never.
- **Salary expectation**: Do not include on resume.

## Length

- **2 pages** is Canadian standard for senior professionals.
- Amarsh: **2 pages**.

## Photo

- **Never include.**

## Work authorization statement

The clearest framing for Canada:

> "Open to relocation to Canada; require employer-sponsored work permit (LMIA-exempt ICT or CUSMA/USMCA route, or employer-supported Express Entry)."

Note: "LMIA-exempt" signals you know the fast path. Many US/Canadian companies can hire under CUSMA (Canada-US-Mexico Agreement) for certain tech roles, bypassing the standard Labour Market Impact Assessment.

For simpler communication:
> Location: Bengaluru, India → Targeting Toronto / Vancouver | Require work permit sponsorship

## Format

- **Paper**: Letter (8.5 × 11 inches) — Canada follows US paper size, unlike most other countries
- **Canadian English**: Mix of UK and US spellings; "organized" (US) and "colour" (UK) both accepted. Be consistent.
- **Date format**: Month DD, YYYY (May 29, 2026) or YYYY-MM-DD (ISO, common in tech)

## ATS / portals

- **Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby**: Shopify, Cohere, Wattpad use these. Scannable via scan.mjs.
- **LinkedIn CA**: Primary platform for Toronto and Vancouver tech hiring. Manual apply.
- **Indeed CA**: High volume. Use for broad scan.
- **Workopolis**: Canada-specific board; declining but still used by some employers.
- **Job Bank (canada.ca)**: Government board. Low signal for AI specialist roles but useful for NOC verification.

## Summary framing

Lead with impact, close with Canada interest + work permit:

```
Senior AI Engineer with 6+ years delivering production agentic systems, LLM platforms, and enterprise
GenAI automation. At OpenText: AI Cockpit (multi-agent orchestration, 10x delivery acceleration) and
LangGraph self-healing CI/CD migration agent (12x faster, hackathon winner, 10+ teams adopted).
Specialised in agentic AI, RAG, MCP tooling, and LLMOps. Open to relocation to Toronto or Vancouver;
require employer-sponsored work permit.
```

Key CA market phrases:
- Toronto and Vancouver are Canada's main AI hubs (Vector Institute, Mila connection)
- "production ML systems" and "LLMOps" are in high demand at Canadian scale-ups
- Cohere is Toronto-based — **highest priority Canadian AI company for this profile**
- "responsible AI" framing is valued in Canada (Canadian AI governance culture)

## Express Entry note (for cover letter, not resume)

Applying for roles in Canada also builds your Express Entry profile. Once you have a job offer, you may qualify for an LMIA-exempt work permit under C10 (significant benefit to Canada) or C11. With 1 year of Canadian work experience → eligible for Canadian Experience Class → PR in 6 months. Worth mentioning in interviews: signals long-term commitment.

## Red flags to avoid

- Do NOT include a photo
- Do NOT use A4 format — Canada uses Letter size
- Do NOT say "H-1B" or any US immigration term
- Do NOT omit the work permit statement — Canadian employers are used to sponsoring but need it flagged upfront
- Do NOT list salary expectations on resume
