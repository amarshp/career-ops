# Resume Norms: Germany
Last updated: 2026-05-29
Source: BAMF EU Blue Card guidelines, German tech recruiter practice, make-it-in-germany.com

## Mandatory inclusions

- **Visa sponsorship note**: "Require EU Blue Card sponsorship (IT shortage occupation — €45,934 threshold)."
- **Current location + relocation intent**: "Bengaluru, India — open to relocation to Berlin / Munich / Germany."
- **LinkedIn / XING URL**: Both LinkedIn and XING are used in Germany. Include LinkedIn; XING is optional.
- **GitHub / portfolio**: Important for AI/ML roles, especially at startups.
- **Degree recognition note**: German employers want to know if your Indian degree is recognised. Add: "B.Tech/M.Tech degree — AICTE-accredited, recognized under anabin database (Germany)." Indian IT degrees from major universities are generally recognised via the anabin lookup tool.

## Prohibited / omit

- **Photo**: Optional in Germany (was historically expected, now considered discriminatory pre-screening). **Default: omit.** Including a photo is no longer standard practice and can actually reduce your chances at modern German companies.
- **Date of birth**: Omit. Germany's AGG (Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz) anti-discrimination law makes age discrimination illegal.
- **Marital status / religion / nationality**: Omit.
- **Salary expectation**: Do not include on resume (Gehaltsvorstellung goes in cover letter if requested).

## Length

- **2 pages** is the German standard for experienced professionals.
- German resumes (Lebenslauf) traditionally have a specific format — but for international applications at German tech companies (Berlin startups, Munich enterprise), a Western-format resume is fine.
- Amarsh: **2 pages, Western format**.

## Photo

- **Omit.** Modern German tech companies (Aleph Alpha, Helsing, Zalando, Personio) do not expect or want photos on resumes. Traditional German corporate culture (banks, automotive) historically expected them, but AI/tech roles have moved away from this.

## Work authorization statement

Add to summary or header:

> "Require EU Blue Card sponsorship (IT specialist, shortage occupation pathway — €45,934 threshold)."

This is important: explicitly naming the Blue Card and threshold signals you've done your homework. German HR teams appreciate specificity.

## Format

- **Paper**: A4
- **Language**: English for international/startup roles; German expected at traditional German companies. Most AI roles at tech companies operate in English — submit in English unless the job posting is in German.
- **Cover letter**: More important in Germany than elsewhere. A 1-page Anschreiben or an equivalent email cover note is expected. It should mention: why this company, why Germany, Blue Card eligibility.

## ATS / portals

- **Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby**: Used by Berlin/Munich AI-native startups (Aleph Alpha, Helsing, Celonis).
- **Stepstone.de**: Germany's largest job board. AI roles listed here. Supports English search.
- **Xing Jobs**: Significant in Germany, especially for corporate roles.
- **Indeed.de**: Works in German and English.
- **Arbeitsagentur (Federal Employment Agency)**: Government portal. Less relevant for AI specialist roles.
- **LinkedIn Germany**: Active but blocked for automated use. Flag for manual.

## The Language Question

- For Berlin startup roles: **English-only resume + English cover letter** is fine.
- For Munich enterprise roles (BMW, Siemens, SAP AI): **German cover letter + English resume** is optimal.
- Learning German (B1) accelerates PR: mention "currently learning German" only if genuinely true.

## Summary framing

Lead with production impact, close with Blue Card + Germany interest:

```
Senior AI Engineer with 6+ years delivering production agentic systems, LLM platforms, and enterprise
GenAI automation. At OpenText: AI Cockpit (multi-agent orchestration, 10x delivery acceleration,
expert agents with RBAC, LangSmith tracing) and LangGraph self-healing CI/CD migration agent
(12x faster, hackathon winner, adopted by 10+ teams). Specialised in agentic AI, RAG, MCP tooling,
and LLMOps. Open to relocation to Germany; eligible for EU Blue Card (IT shortage occupation).
```

Key DE market phrases:
- "enterprise-grade" — German companies love this framing
- "Zuverlässigkeit" (reliability) is implicitly valued — quantified metrics prove it
- "Mittelstand AI" awareness if applying to traditional companies (the 99% of Germany that isn't a startup)
- "GDPR-compliant AI systems" — strong signal for German enterprise clients

## Red flags to avoid

- Do NOT include a photo
- Do NOT use US Letter format
- Do NOT say "need visa" — say "EU Blue Card eligible (IT shortage occupation)"
- Do NOT omit a cover letter if applying to a traditional German company — it signals poor cultural fit
- Do NOT write only in German if you can't write it fluently — broken German is worse than English
