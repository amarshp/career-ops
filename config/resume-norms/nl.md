# Resume Norms: Netherlands
Last updated: 2026-05-29
Source: IND Highly Skilled Migrant guidelines, Dutch tech recruiter practice, 30% ruling documentation

## Mandatory inclusions

- **Visa sponsorship note**: "Require Dutch Highly Skilled Migrant (HSM) permit or EU Blue Card sponsorship."
- **Current location + relocation intent**: "Hyderabad, India — open to relocation to Amsterdam / Netherlands."
- **LinkedIn URL**: Dutch tech recruiters lean heavily on LinkedIn (Netherlands has one of highest LinkedIn penetration rates globally).
- **GitHub / portfolio links**: Important for technical roles.
- **English proficiency**: Implicit from the resume being in English — most Dutch tech companies operate in English.

## Prohibited / omit

- **Photo**: Optional in Netherlands (not banned, not required). Default to **omitting** for ATS submissions. Only include if specifically invited to.
- **BSN (citizen service number)**: Never — only needed post-hire.
- **Date of birth**: Not required; omit.
- **Salary expectation**: Do not include.
- **Marital status / religion**: Never.

## Length

- **1–2 pages**. Dutch recruiters are efficient; 2 pages is fine for senior, 3 is too long.
- Amarsh: **2 pages**.

## Photo

- **Omit by default.** Dutch anti-discrimination norms (Wet gelijke behandeling) discourage photo-based pre-screening even though it's not legally prohibited.

## Work authorization statement

Add to summary or header:

> "Require Dutch Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) permit — eligible for fast-track processing via IND-recognised sponsor."

Note: emphasising "fast-track via IND-recognised sponsor" is useful — it signals you know the process and reduces perceived friction for HR.

## Format

- **Paper**: A4
- **Language**: English — virtually all Amsterdam/Rotterdam tech companies work in English
- **Date format**: DD-MM-YYYY (Dutch convention) or month-name format

## The 30% Ruling — mention in cover letter, not resume

The 30% ruling (expat tax benefit, first 5 years) is available for Amarsh's salary level. Mentioning it in a cover letter signals awareness of Dutch expat onboarding. Example:
> "I am aware of and eligible for the 30% ruling, which simplifies the relocation package discussion."
Do NOT put this in the resume itself.

## ATS / portals

- **Greenhouse / Ashby**: Booking.com, Adyen, Mollie, Takeaway.com all use these.
- **Workday**: ASML, Philips, Shell use Workday. Paste + upload PDF.
- **Indeed.nl**: Works in Dutch AND English. High volume; use English filter.
- **Nationalevacaturebank**: Dutch generalist board. Less signal for AI roles.
- **LinkedIn Netherlands**: Very active. Most Amsterdam tech jobs are on LinkedIn. Flag for manual apply.

## Summary framing

Lead with production systems, close with NL relocation + HSM note:

```
Senior AI Engineer with 6+ years building production agentic systems, LLM platforms, and enterprise
GenAI automation at scale. At OpenText: AI Cockpit (multi-agent orchestration, 10x delivery, LangSmith
tracing) and LangGraph self-healing CI/CD migration agent (12x acceleration, 10+ teams adopted).
Deep expertise in RAG, MCP tooling, LLMOps, and applied ML. Available to relocate to the Netherlands;
require Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) permit sponsorship.
```

Key NL market phrases:
- "scale" — Amsterdam tech companies value scale-thinking (Booking, Adyen, ASML operate at massive scale)
- "data-driven" — Dutch tech culture is extremely metrics-focused
- "open-source contributions" — Dutch tech community values this (link PersonaRAG, DevOps Copilot)
- "async / distributed team" — many NL tech companies have global distributed teams

## Red flags to avoid

- Do NOT submit in Dutch unless specifically asked — English is the working language
- Do NOT include a photo (see above)
- Do NOT overload the Skills section — Dutch tech culture values depth over breadth
- Do NOT use US Letter format
- Avoid the framing "willing to learn Dutch" — it can signal short-term mindset. Instead: "Learning Dutch, currently at A1" if true, or stay silent on language
